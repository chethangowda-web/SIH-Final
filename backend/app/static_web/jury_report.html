1. Problem Statement & Breakthrough

India's National Food Security Act (NFSA) delivers food security to 800+ million citizens through 5.4 lakh Fair Price Shops (FPS). Traditionally, foodgrain is allocated using static decennial census records.

Under the One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC) portability framework, citizens have the legal right to lift grain anywhere in India. However, rapid urban migration (e.g., workers migrating from rural districts into Bengaluru Urban) causes severe stockouts at migrant-dense shops while rural shops sit on perishable surplus grain.

PDS DemandSync is an intelligent, closed-loop decision-support overlay that solves this crisis by capturing voluntary early intent, applying multi-variable predictive machine learning, solving vehicle routing problems (CVRP), and locking transactions with cryptographic tamper-evidence.

Frontend Innovations: Native instant switching between Kannada (ಕನ್ನಡ), Hindi (हिन्दी), and English. Features zero-typing commodity iconography (Rice, Wheat, Coarse Grains) designed for illiterate and rural cardholders.

Entitlement Engine: Computes legal NFSA entitlement: Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) fixed 35 kg ($21\text{ kg}$ Rice, $14\text{ kg}$ Wheat) vs. Priority Household (PHH) at 5 kg/person/month.

Non-Binding Statutory Guarantee: Citizens who fail to declare intent or cannot visit the shop never forfeit their legal entitlement. The grain remains protected by the 5% FPS safety buffer.

5. Master Jury Q&A Defense Script

Q1: "Why not use a simple 3-month moving average instead of Machine Learning?"
Defense: A historical moving average is purely backward-looking. In Karnataka, when 400 construction workers suddenly migrate from Raichur into Whitefield, Bengaluru for infrastructure work, a historical average would allocate grain to empty villages in Raichur and under-allocate in Whitefield, causing instant stockouts. PDS DemandSync uses historical EMA as a baseline, but dynamically blends it with early voluntary intent signals weighted by historical adherence reliability ($C_j$), shifting governance from reactive crisis management to proactive positioning.
Q2: "What prevents an FPS dealer from submitting fake intents to hoard grain?"
Defense: Two layers of defense: 1) The intent_signals table enforces a composite unique constraint UNIQUE(card_id, cycle_id), preventing duplicate submissions. 2) Our Confidence Scoring Engine monitors historical adherence. If declared intents do not materialize into biometric Aadhaar liftings on the e-PoS machine, that shop's confidence score ($C_j$) drops toward zero. The forecast engine automatically discounts unverified intent back down to historical baselines.
Q3: "How do you prevent a corrupt officer from altering database rows directly?"
Defense: Zero-trust cryptographic audit trails. When the DSO approves an allocation, the manifest is serialized into canonical JSON and sealed with a SHA-256 cryptographic digest stored in an append-only governance log. If an insider modifies rows via raw SQL, the recomputed digest in the Auditor Workspace will mismatch the sealed digest. The portal immediately triggers a crimson TAMPER DETECTED alarm.
Q4: "How is the Field Food Inspector's physical presence guaranteed?"
Defense: The inspector portal uses the Haversine geodesic formula against the state GIS master database coordinates. Arrival is blocked if the device is $> 50\text{ meters}$ away. Furthermore, presence is validated by querying the shop's physical electronic Point of Sale (e-PoS) terminal via a live telemetry ping ($142\text{ ms}$ latency), making remote fabrication technically impossible.
Q5: "How does the system scale to an entire state with 20,000 shops?"
Defense: The architecture is decoupled by district. Each district (e.g., Bengaluru Urban, Mysuru, Belagavi) runs its optimization cycle independently. The Clarke-Wright vehicle routing heuristic runs in $\mathcal{O}(n^2 \log n)$ time—solving 100+ delivery nodes in $<1.2\text{s}$. The FastAPI backend is fully asynchronous (ASGI), running on containerized clusters with SQLAlchemy ORM allowing instant migration from SQLite to distributed PostgreSQL or CockroachDB.
